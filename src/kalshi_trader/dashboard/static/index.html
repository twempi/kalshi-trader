Opportunity scanner

Latest state of every opportunity evaluated in the last 15 minutes. Size, fill price, fees and expected profit are computed for the actual simulated order (book walked at that size). "Fair (low)" is the conservative model probability used for decisions. "observed" = would have been allocated but the strategy runs in observe mode. Max risk = maximum possible loss.

Strategy comparison

Main = the shared paper account (central allocator, unvalidated strategies at reduced size). Shadow = each strategy's own standalone ledger, trading every approved opportunity with identical risk rules, so strategies are compared on the same observations. Expected = sum of ex-ante conservative expected P&L at the actual fills. All values simulated.

Capital allocation and exposure (main account)

Exposure is maximum possible loss of open positions, against the configured caps.

Why is the system (not) trading?

Outcome counts for every opportunity evaluated today (UTC), and the most common reasons for rejection. "Last cycle" = the most recent one-second evaluation. Zero trades is an acceptable outcome when nothing clears the requirements.

Tennis model vs market (forward data)

Model probabilities recorded live next to the executable market, scored once markets settle. Benchmark = market-implied probability (YES mid). Each match counts once (event-weighted).

Market making - research simulation

Hypothetical quotes and pessimistic simulated maker fills (fill only when the market trades through our price). Not paper trades and not real fills.

Tennis match-winner markets

Prices are real Kalshi quotes (YES side). Market prob = mid of bid/ask. Model prob = Markov estimate; "robust" = lower credible bound after assuming the score feed lags by the configured number of points. EV = robust prob − ask, before fees (fees and slippage depend on the final order size; see the Opportunities tab). Rows tinted = an opportunity was approved, observed or filled.

Is it skill or luck?

Null hypothesis: every contract wins with probability equal to its entry price (the market is calibrated). Small samples cannot distinguish skill from randomness.
